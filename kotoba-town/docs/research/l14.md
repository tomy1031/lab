| g1 | Vて (て-form) | Group I: い・ち・り→って, み・び・に→んで, き→いて, ぎ→いで, し→して (exception いきます→いって); Group II: ます→て; Group III: して, きて |

## Real-life situations
- Asking station staff or a stranger for help (how to buy a ticket, how to read a word, speak slowly).
- Offering help to a busy colleague, a boss or a neighbour — and answering such an offer.
- Reporting what you or someone else is doing right now.

## Common learner mistakes
- The wrong group I ending: ✗ まちて, ✗ よみて, ✗ かきて, ✗ はなして→はなって; ✗ いきて／いいて for いって.
- Treating group I verbs that end in -えります／-いります as group II (✗ かえて for かえって, ✗ はいて for はいって),
  and the other way round for short group II verbs (みます, います, おきます).
- Not knowing the verbs well enough before conjugating — the Japan Foundation KL workshop (teachers in
  Malaysia) names this, grouping, and the group I changes as the main difficulties, and recommends lots of
  classroom ～て ください before the rule is taught.
- In speech, dropping the small っ or the ん (まて ください, よで ください). Khmer and other South-East Asian
  languages have no length contrast of this kind, so the scenes make the learner say まって, とって, てつだって,
  よんで, かけて, かいて aloud. (Author's teaching knowledge; no Cambodia-specific study was found.)
- ～ましょう (let's) where ～ましょうか (shall I?) is meant, and answering an offer with a bare はい／いいえ.
- ～て ください to a superior without a softener; forgetting すみませんが.
- を instead of が in あめが ふって います.

Level check: NPC lines use the て-form only in ～て ください and ～て います (progress). No ～ても／～ては／～てから,
no て-chaining of clauses, no state ～て います, no ない-form, no plain style. 入れます／押します (lesson 16) are avoided:
the attendant says いっしょに 買いましょう and 240円を 出して ください instead of explaining the buttons.

## Layout assumptions for the scene builders
- `station`: the learner stands at `ticket_machine` with 竹内 beside them (`npc1`); 岡田 (`staff`) points at the
  machine, at `platform_sign_2` and the `train` arrives at the end. さくらまち is a made-up station, 240 yen away.
- `office`: 青木 (`npc1`) has a stack of paper; 小川 (`npc2`) walks in at n3. The lines mention a shelf (たな) with
  copy paper and a copier — neither is a catalogued prop, so nothing points at them.
- `apartment`: the learner holds a paper slip (not a catalogued prop; `mailbox` is the focus). あや is at her
  door (`npc1`), しゅん comes out behind her (`npc2`) at n5. あや makes the call on her mobile.
