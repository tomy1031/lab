# Lesson 20 — research notes

## What the lesson teaches
| id | pattern | note |
|---|---|---|
| g1 | plain style: verbs | いく／いかない／いった／いかなかった replace 〜ます／〜ません／〜ました／〜ませんでした |
| g2 | plain style: adjectives and nouns | い-adj drops です (おいしい, おいしかった, おいしくない); な-adj and nouns take だ／だった／じゃ ない／じゃ なかった |
| g3 | casual questions | no か, rising intonation; だ is dropped (ひま？ どうだった？) |
| g4 | うん／ううん, dropped particles | は, を, へ are often left out; で, に, から, まで, と stay |
| g5 | 〜けど | casual "but" (the polite counterpart is 〜が) |
| g6 | choosing the style | plain with close friends and family; です／ます with a manager, teacher, staff, strangers |

Also in the lesson (used in NPC lines, not a separate id): だ dropped or softened with よ／ね in statements
(ジュースだよ, 強かったね), casual invitations with 〜ない？, and ぼく.

## Real-life situations
- Eating, drinking and making plans with friends: 何 飲む？ 見た？ 行かない？
- Giving a quick opinion: おいしい, むずかしいけど 楽しい.
- The moment a senior joins: the same person answers a friend with うん、終わった and the manager with
  はい、書きました.

## When plain style is right, and when it is rude
- Right: close friends, family, classmates of the same age, people who have started using it with you.
- Rude or childish: a manager, a teacher, shop or station staff, older people, strangers — also when *they* speak
  casually to you (a manager's ありがとう does not invite うん in return).
- Not wrong but distant: keeping です／ます with a close friend who has switched to plain style. The branches
  accept such answers; the judge is asked to tip that they sound distant.
- When unsure, です／ます is always the safe choice. If plain style slips out to a senior: あ、すみません, and
  switch back.

## Common learner mistakes
- Keeping か or だ in a casual question: ✗ 行くか？ ✗ ひまだ？ → ○ 行く？ ○ ひま？
- だ after an い-adjective: ✗ おいしいだ → ○ おいしい; forgetting だった／じゃ ない for nouns and な-adjectives.
- Wrong plain forms: ✗ いいた → ○ いった; ✗ あらない → ○ ない; ✗ いくない → ○ よくない; ✗ かあない → ○ かわない;
  ✗ 見ないだった → ○ 見なかった.
- Mixing styles inside one answer (うん、行きます) or attaching です to a plain verb (行くです).
- Dropping particles that must stay (で, に, と, から, まで).
- Carrying plain style over to seniors once it has been learnt — the typical "sounds rude" slip of learners who
  pick up Japanese from anime and friends — and the opposite, never leaving です／ます.
- Learners from Cambodia and neighbouring countries know speech levels from their own languages (they are shown by
  pronouns and special words), so the idea is familiar; what is new is that Japanese shows the level on *every*
  sentence ending. (Author's teaching knowledge; no Khmer-specific study was found.)

`whenEn` says that a polite answer takes the same branch but should get a "sounds distant with a friend" tip; the
two manager turns in l20c say that a plain answer is rude there. `exampleJa`, `hintJa` and `modelAnswers` are plain
style in casual turns and です／ます in the manager turns.
