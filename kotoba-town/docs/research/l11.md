# Lesson 11 — research notes

## What the lesson teaches
| id | pattern | note |
|---|---|---|
| g1 | ひとつ・ふたつ … とお／いくつ | native numbers for counting things up to ten; from 11 the ordinary numbers; いくつ asks how many |
| g2 | number + counter（～人・～枚・～台・～回）／何人・何枚 | ～にん people (ひとり, ふたり, よにん are irregular), ～まい flat things, ～だい machines and vehicles, ～かい times |
| g3 | N を／が quantity V | the quantity stands right before the verb and takes no particle (きってを 5まい かいました) |
| g4 | ～時間・～日・～週間・～か月・～年 | lengths of time, also without a particle before the verb |
| g5 | どのくらい ～か／～ かかります | how long / how much; かかります for time and money |
| g6 | period に ～回 V | frequency: 1しゅうかんに 2かい |
| g7 | ～ぐらい／～だけ | about / only |

Also in the lesson (used in NPC lines, not a separate id): ぜんぶで, family words (りょうしん, きょうだい, あに, あね,
おとうと, いもうと and the polite ご家族, ご兄弟), います for "I have (brothers, children)" and "I am in Japan".
～台 is listed in g2 and the vocabulary but no learner turn needs it; the scenes had no natural place for machines.

## Real-life situations
- Buying several of something in a shop (おにぎりを みっつ, きってを 5まい).
- Sending a parcel and asking how long it takes or how much it costs.
- Family talk: how many people, how many brothers and sisters, how often you call home, how far away home is.
- Getting-to-know-you questions at school: how long, how many hours a day, how many times a week.

## Common learner mistakes
- Irregular readings: ひとり／ふたり (✗ いちにん, ✗ ににん), よにん (✗ よんにん), むっつ／やっつ written or said without the small っ,
  いっかい, いっかげつ, いっしゅうかん, じゅっぷん.
- A particle after the quantity: ✗ きってを 5まいを かいました, ✗ がくせいが ふたりが います. Also the textbook-English order
  ✗ 5まいの きってを ください where the plain "N を quantity" is wanted.
- に after a length of time: ✗ 1ねんに べんきょうしました (に belongs to points in time, 7じに), and the reverse, dropping に in the
  frequency pattern: ✗ 1しゅうかん 2かい.
- あります for family members (✗ あにが ひとり あります) — carried over from lesson 10.
- ぐらい placed before the number, and だけ confused with しか (not yet taught; だけ takes an affirmative verb).
- あに／あね for someone else's family, or おにいさん for one's own, when speaking to outsiders.
- For Cambodian and other South-East Asian learners: Khmer has no case particles and the number follows the noun with an
  often optional classifier, so the two weak spots are where the particle goes (on the noun, never on the number) and
  remembering to use a counter at all (✗ きってを 5 ください). This is our own inference from the structure of Khmer; we found
  no published error study on Khmer speakers for this lesson.

## Layout assumptions for the scene builders
- `konbini`: rice balls (`onigiri`) are within the learner's reach; stamps and postcards are kept at the `register`.
  The learner's parcel is not a prop — the clerk only mentions it.
- `classroom`: a few chocolates (`chocolate`) lie on the learner's desk; Ana sits at `npc1` and points at them.
- `home`: no prop is referred to; dinner (curry) is announced at the end.
